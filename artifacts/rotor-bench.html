The r throws its arms

A rotate has no single heading — it acts all the way round — so it throws four beams at once and then turns a quarter as it goes. The turn is the tell: the piece does to itself the thing it does to the board. Everything the beams do is shared with the push beam; what is new here is how many, how they are aimed, and the turn the glyph makes on its way out.

Four beams, always four — that is how many neighbours the rules take round. Each one
  ends on the live position of the piece it has hold of, so its direction and its speed are the
  piece's rather than a circle drawn around the cell. grip is how far along it
  reaches: at 100 the tip sits on the piece, below that it stops short, above that it overshoots.
  The beams draw under the pieces, so a piece reads as held rather than painted over. This page
  runs the game's own renderer over the turn the rules actually resolve.
