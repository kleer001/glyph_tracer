Glyph Tracer · everything from one pass

Five motions, recorded

A won level clearing itself away, a piece squashing as it is shoved, the tray reporting how far the board has drained, what a cleared cell throws, and the answer to a swap that achieved nothing. Every one is the real engine resolving the real rules.

What changed underneath

A dying cell's turn stopped being derived. It used to run on a curve worked out at each frame from how much room the shrink had made, which guaranteed a corner never crossed into the cell beside it. The squash needs those corners — narrowing to 60% along the travel means stretching to 1.667 across, which is wider than a cell — so the guarantee went and the curve became a number in the data file. Pieces bulge over their neighbours now, on purpose.

The finale commits the level itself. Growing the next board in and then waiting for a tap does not work: the moment playback ends, the board being held is still the spent one, and it paints straight over the arrival. So the level advances when the finale ends rather than when you next touch it.

A burst is a closed form, not a simulation. Every particle's whole life is a function of how long ago its beat began. Nothing is stepped, so nothing has to be reset when playback is cut short, and one seed throws the same burst every time.

One thing still wrong

A burst cannot outlive the beat its cell dies on, because that beat is where its clock starts. At the shipped timings that beat is 300 ms, so the burst is capped there rather than finishing — at 620 it was being cut off at less than half its life, while still bright. Letting it run longer needs a clock that survives a phase boundary, which is a change to what a frame carries rather than a number to turn.

How these were made

The frame harness draws one named effect at an elapsed time it is handed and runs no clock of its own, so a capture is the same frame every run. That matters more than it sounds: a canvas screenshot costs seconds here, so anything timed against a wall clock finishes before the first frame is taken.
