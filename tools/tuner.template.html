Three motions, on sliders

A swap that bows off its own straight line, the copies it leaves behind it, and the shove the board takes when an ability fires. Everything below is the game's own code, inlined — not a mock-up of it — so a number that looks right here is a number that goes straight into the file.

smear

What the board is doing

  
A piece is swapped clear across the board onto its own colour, fires, and sets off
  whatever the rules set off. The swap is the long travel the arc and the smear are for;
  the firing is what shakes the board. Press next board for another deal
  — every one is seeded, so the same board comes back in the same order.

  
The three

  
The arc. Two pieces trading along one row travel the same line in
  opposite directions and pass through each other. Each bows to the side its own heading
  turns to, so their bows are opposite and one goes clear of the other. At zero they are
  back to passing through.

  
The smear. Copies of the piece strung back along the path it took,
  fading out. They sit on the arc, not on the straight line, so the two knobs read
  together. Below least distance a swap leaves none — a one-cell swap has
  nothing worth following.

  
The shake. The board is shoved on the beat an ability fires and
  settles back. Only the board: the readouts above it are pinned and do not move, and the
  cell under your finger is still the cell you tapped. Deeper links in a chain shake
  harder, capped where the chain's build is capped.
