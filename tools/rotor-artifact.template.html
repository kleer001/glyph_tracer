The r throws its arms

A rotate has no single heading — it acts all the way round — so it throws four beams at once and then turns a quarter as it goes. The turn is the tell: the piece does to itself the thing it does to the board. Everything the beams do is shared with the push beam; what is new here is how many, how they are aimed, and the turn the glyph makes on its way out.

aim turns the whole set of arms, so four arms at an aim of 45 point at the corners
  instead of the edges. sweep turns each arm as it travels, which is what makes the set
  read as a pinwheel rather than a cross. stagger delays each arm behind the one before,
  so they leave in sequence. The turn out is the glyph's own quarter: a mirrored r
  takes it the other way, because the piece turns the way its ability does. This page runs the game's
  own renderer, and the beam maths is src/fx.js.
