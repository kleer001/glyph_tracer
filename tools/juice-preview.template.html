The flash brightens toward white

The same recording three times, one knob apart. A piece lands on its own colour, shoves the line in front of it, and its cell goes. The third is what ships: for a fifth of a second the cell it landed on travels part of the way to white.

Measured on the recordings above, at the brightest frame, on the cell that fired: additive took 41,136,0 to 62,206,0 — one channel driven, two stuck. Toward white it goes to 119,179,93, with no channel clipped. The first saturates; the second lightens.

What the flash is

A radial bloom of white composited with screen, at half strength, loudest at the landing and spent 180 ms later. It draws over the ground and under everything else, so a beam and a piece both pass in front of it.

screen with white is arithmetically the distance from a colour to white: each channel moves the same fraction of the headroom it has left. Nothing can clip, by construction, and no colour is left out.

A bloom rather than a filled cell, because the tiles have rounded corners and sit in gutters: a square of added light would put four bright corners in the gaps between cells, which is precisely where nothing happened. It reaches __SPREAD__ of a cell width, which keeps it off the neighbours — a wider one lights up cells that did not do anything.

Why not brighten toward its own colour

Because the palette does not take it evenly. Adding a colour to itself brightens it only where that colour has headroom, and this palette leaves wildly different amounts. Every live colour at the flash's loudest instant, additively:

Lift is in relative luminance, and the spread is sevenfold. A green cell floods; a red one barely moves, because red's own channel is already at 243 and adding more of it clips on the first frame, leaving only the two channels that are not the colour to do anything.

That is worse than being weak. A feedback signal is meant to report the event, and this one reports the hue of the tile the event happened on: the same activation is a flood on cyan and a shrug on red.

Threefold, and red goes from worst to nearly best. What is left of the spread is physical rather than a choice of blend mode: yellow already sits at 0.79 luminance and has almost nowhere bright to go. No arrangement of light fixes that, and a table of six per-colour alphas would be six numbers spent buying an evenness the eye was not asking for.

The other thing the numbers do not say: toward white the cell desaturates, where additive saturates. A red tile flashing additively goes a more livid red; toward white it goes pink. Pink is the one that reads as light falling on a surface rather than the surface changing colour.

And the board sat down into a tray

The one that is about the mechanic rather than the feel.

Tiles used to float on the paper, which made a cleared cell a hole in the page. The grid now sits in a recessed tray, and the same hole is a hole in the board. For a game whose whole premise is that the board drains, that is the difference between emptiness reading as progress and emptiness reading as nothing rendered.

The tray is six numbers under well in data/gloss.json: how far it reaches past the outermost cells, how much darker than the paper it is, and the shadow cast inward from its rim.

It matters more here than it would elsewhere, and the palette is why: a yellow tile against the bare paper measures 1.02:1. They are the same luminance. Before the tray, the gutter and the tile's own shadow were the only things separating a pale cell from a gap where a cell used to be.

What is still on the shelf

  • A particle burst on a clear — needs a layer and a clock, and the clock is on the frame now. Small particles, radial rather than falling.
  • Feedback for a dud swap — a swap that achieves nothing currently produces nothing. It has to read as legal but pointless, because the pieces really do trade and the budget really does go down.
  • Move preview — declined. The game does not show the future board state. A deterministic cascade makes it cheap to build, and that was never the argument: the player learns the cascade by playing it.
  • Segment welding — declined. Priced cheap by analogy with a sister game that welds consecutive moves of one piece. Here a destruction beat sits between every pair of movement beats, so welding means rebuilding the timeline as per-piece envelopes instead of phases — and it pulls against the freeze and the chain build, which exist to make each link a distinct beat.

How these were made

Both recordings are the real engine resolving the real rules. The frame harness draws one named effect at an elapsed time it is handed and runs no clock of its own, so a capture is the same frame every run — which is what makes one recording worth comparing against the next. The two above differ by a single number, substituted at the network layer so nothing in the game moved between the passes.

The two rejected recordings are each shown at the strength it was judged at, not at a shared number — an effect argued down at its own best setting has been argued down properly.
