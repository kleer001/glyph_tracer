Glyph Tracer · every ability, once

What each symbol does when it goes

Eleven recordings, one per effect — the four pushes share theirs, and so do the pairs that differ only in which way they lean. Every one is the real engine resolving the real rules: the shove, the trade, the turn and the swallow are all the board's answer, and the effect is drawn over the top of whatever it decided.

The two rules underneath

A beam only goes where there is a piece. An ability reaching into an empty cell, a hole, or off the board does nothing there, and a beam drawn into that space would be claiming otherwise. So the same glyph throws four beams mid-board and one in a drained corner. That is information, not an inconsistency.

A thrown beam and a held beam are different things. A shove sends a piece away, so its beam leaves, runs its reach and dissipates; it never depended on hitting anything. A swap, a turn and a sink all take hold of a piece and move it, so their tips land on it and stay with it. Backwards, a thrown beam would read as pulling the piece it is meant to be shoving.

Two things follow from those. A beam draws behind the pieces, so a held piece reads as held rather than painted over; a ghost draws in front, because it is coming off the piece itself and a tenth-bigger outline behind the glyph is hidden by the glyph that cast it. And no piece gets both: a beam already says what happened, and a ghost saying it again reads as two separate events.

The piece itself leaves before its cell does. It fades while the ability is still travelling, and the ground it sat on keeps turning and shrinking without it.
